Overview

Spark plasma sintering (SPS) consolidates powder into a dense component by passing electric current through it under mechanical pressure. It is a key process in advanced materials manufacturing, and it is hard to simulate well: what happens between individual powder particles determines the outcome, but resolving those particles across a full component is computationally prohibitive.

Existing finite element models sit at one of two extremes. Continuum models treat the powder as a homogeneous medium and lose particle-level detail entirely. Particle-based methods capture that detail but cannot scale to realistic geometries. This project developed a Direct FE² multiscale framework that closes the gap — microscale powder behaviour is integrated directly into macroscale electro-thermo-mechanical simulation, without the cost of resolving it everywhere.

Problem & motivation

SPS couples electrical, thermal, and mechanical physics across scales that differ by orders of magnitude. Current flows preferentially through particle contacts, generating localised Joule heating, which softens the material, which changes the contact geometry, which changes the current path. The loop is genuinely coupled and genuinely multiscale.

The gap: continuum models ignore particle-level detail; particle-based methods are accurate but computationally intractable at component scale. Neither could simulate large-scale SPS with both accuracy and efficiency.

Contributions

  • Designed a Direct FE² approach for fully coupled electro-thermo-mechanical SPS simulation.
  • Achieved temperature and displacement error below 1% against full FE analysis.
  • Reduced computational cost by factors up to 70×, with degrees of freedom cut up to 44×.
  • Demonstrated flexibility across powder morphologies — simple cubic, BCC, and FCC packings.
  • Validated the densification response against the Heckel equation.

Method

Solver

Abaqus + Python

Coupled electro-thermo-mechanical FE models, with Python scripting automating mesh generation, RVE embedding, and constraint assembly.

Coupling

Direct FE²

Representative volume elements attached at macroscale integration points via multi-point constraints — no separate microscale solve, no nested iteration.

Boundary

Periodic conditions

Periodic boundary conditions on each RVE enforce scale separation while preserving the microscale deformation and current fields.

Material

Johnson–Cook

Rate- and temperature-dependent plasticity for copper powder, capturing softening as Joule heating raises local temperature.

Results

The Direct FE² framework produced results nearly identical to full FE simulation while drastically lowering computational cost. Speedup scales with problem size — the larger and more realistic the geometry, the greater the advantage.

Metric Full FE Direct FE² Improvement
Runtime baseline 8–70× faster up to 70×
Degrees of freedom baseline up to 44× fewer up to 44×
Temperature error — < 1% < 1%
Displacement error — < 1% < 1%

Beyond the efficiency gain, the framework revealed how powder morphology, mesh scaling, and current intensity each affect densification during SPS — relationships that continuum models cannot express because they have no microstructure to vary.

Impact

The method makes it feasible to simulate realistic SPS geometries that were previously computationally intractable. It advances multiscale modelling in materials processing and provides a foundation for future work on non-spherical powders and complex component geometries.
